# 🌊 DWC — Double West Coast IPA

<div class="article-lead">

DWC stands for **Double West Coast IPA** — a stronger, more intense version of the classic West Coast IPA. Compared with a standard WC IPA, it usually has a higher ABV, a firmer bitterness, and a bigger malt base to support heavier hopping. The core identity stays the same: clear structure, assertive bitterness, and hop character leaning toward pine, citrus, resin, and sometimes tropical fruit.

</div>

---

## 🏯 From WC IPA to DWC

West Coast IPA (WC IPA) is already a hop-forward style: crisp, clear, piney, and firmly bitter. **Double West Coast IPA** keeps that structure, but pushes the strength and concentration further:

| | WC IPA | DWC (Double West Coast IPA) |
|---|---|---|
| ABV | 6–7.5% | 8–10%+ |
| IBU (bitterness) | 50–70 | 70–100+ |
| Hop character | Bold, piney, citrusy | Intense, resinous, layered |
| Malt backbone | Clean, supportive | Stronger — needed to balance |
| Clarity | Crystal clear | Clear to slightly hazy |
| Body | Medium | Medium-full |

In craft beer, "double" usually means a larger grain bill and a higher original gravity, which in turn raise both alcohol and body. In a DWC IPA, that stronger base is what allows the brewer to use a heavier hop load without the beer becoming thin or unbalanced.

## 👅 What Does a DWC IPA Taste Like?

<div class="taste-box">

### 🎨 Appearance
- Clear to lightly hazy — especially when also DDH
- Deep golden to light amber
- White head with good retention

### 👃 Aroma
- **Pronounced hop aroma** — pine resin, grapefruit, citrus peel, and sometimes tropical fruit
- **Resinous depth** — depending on the hop bill, earthy or dank notes can also appear
- Citrus zest and stone fruit underneath
- Clean malt sweetness balances without dominating

### 👄 Flavor
- **Bitterness is a defining feature** — often high, but ideally clean and structured rather than harsh
- Hop flavor can be layered: pine, citrus, resin, grapefruit, and sometimes tropical notes
- **Stronger malt backbone** than a standard WC IPA — enough to support the higher alcohol and hop load
- Dry, resinous finish is common, especially in classic examples
- ABV warmth on the back end

### ✨ Mouthfeel
- **Medium to medium-full body** — fuller than a standard IPA, but still meant to finish relatively dry
- Moderate carbonation helps keep the beer from feeling heavy
- The best examples stay firm and drinkable rather than sticky

</div>

## 🌿 DWC + DDH

Some DWC IPAs are also **DDH (Double Dry Hopped)**, creating a **DDH DWC IPA** — a beer that combines:

- The **structure and bitterness** of a Double West Coast IPA
- The **extra aroma lift** of double dry hopping

The result is usually a beer with stronger hop aroma on top of an already firm, bitter base. Done well, DDH adds brightness and detail without changing the core West Coast structure.

---

## 🍺 Hop Varieties Often Used

DWC IPAs lean heavily on American and Southern Hemisphere hops for their signature profile:

- **Citra** → intense tropical fruit, lime
- **Mosaic** → blueberry, earth, stone fruit
- **Simcoe** → pine, passion fruit, dank earth
- **Galaxy** → tropical, peach, citrus
- **Strata** → passion fruit, strawberry, dank

These hops are often used in combination across the boil, whirlpool, and sometimes dry-hop additions to build a layered profile rather than a one-note bitterness.

---

## 📌 Key Takeaways

- **DWC = Double West Coast IPA** — a stronger, more concentrated West Coast IPA
- It typically has higher **ABV**, firmer bitterness, and a larger malt base than standard WC IPA
- Hop character often leans **piney, citrusy, resinous, and dry**
- Some versions are also **DDH**, which adds more aroma on top of the base style
- Best consumed fresh, while hop aroma and bitterness remain sharp and clean
