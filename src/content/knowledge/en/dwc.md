# 🌊 DWC — Double West Coast IPA

<div class="article-lead">

DWC stands for **Double West Coast IPA** — everything that makes a West Coast IPA great, turned up to maximum. More hops, more bitterness, more ABV, and more complexity. This is a beer for those who believe that if one punch of hop intensity is good, two punches are better.

</div>

---

## 🏯 From WC IPA to DWC

The West Coast IPA (WC IPA) is already one of the most aggressively hopped beer styles — crisp, clear, piney, and assertively bitter. The **Double West Coast IPA** takes that blueprint and scales every parameter:

| | WC IPA | DWC (Double West Coast IPA) |
|---|---|---|
| ABV | 6–7.5% | 8–10%+ |
| IBU (bitterness) | 50–70 | 70–100+ |
| Hop character | Bold, piney, citrusy | Intense, resinous, layered |
| Malt backbone | Clean, supportive | Stronger — needed to balance |
| Clarity | Crystal clear | Clear to slightly hazy |
| Body | Medium | Medium-full |

"Double" in craft beer terminology typically refers to scaling up the grain bill, which raises both the ABV and the malt body — the increased malt provides the platform to support a correspondingly massive hop addition.

## 👅 What Does a DWC IPA Taste Like?

<div class="taste-box">

### 🎨 Appearance
- Clear to lightly hazy — especially when also DDH
- Deep golden to light amber
- White head with good retention

### 👃 Aroma
- **Massive hop presence** — pine resin, tropical fruit, grapefruit
- **Dank, resinous depth** — cannabis-like earthiness is common at this intensity
- Citrus zest and stone fruit underneath
- Clean malt sweetness balances without dominating

### 👄 Flavor
- **Bitterness is the headline** — 70–100+ IBU, but it feels structured, not harsh
- **Multi-layered hop flavor** — the palate keeps finding new notes
- **Stronger malt backbone** than a standard WC IPA — needed to carry the weight
- Dry, resinous finish that lingers with satisfaction
- ABV warmth on the back end

### ✨ Mouthfeel
- **Medium to full body** — substantial without being heavy
- Moderately high carbonation — adds crispness
- Dry finish characteristic of the West Coast style

</div>

## 🌿 DWC + DDH = The Ultimate

Many of the most sought-after DWC IPAs on the market are also **DDH (Double Dry Hopped)**, creating the **DDH DWC IPA** — a beer that combines:

- The **structure and bitterness** of a Double West Coast IPA
- The **aromatic explosion** of double dry hopping

The result is a beer that hits you with aroma the moment it's poured, then delivers a layered, complex bitterness that hangs around long after the sip. It's not a session beer — it's a meditation in hops.

---

## 🍺 Hop Varieties Often Used

DWC IPAs lean heavily on American and Southern Hemisphere hops for their signature profile:

- **Citra** → intense tropical fruit, lime
- **Mosaic** → blueberry, earth, stone fruit
- **Simcoe** → pine, passion fruit, dank earth
- **Galaxy** → tropical, peach, citrus
- **Strata** → passion fruit, strawberry, dank

These are often used in combination — layered across the boil and multiple dry-hop additions — to build the complex, multidimensional hop profile a great DWC demands.

## 📌 Key Takeaways

- **DWC = Double West Coast IPA** — a bigger, more intense WC IPA
- Higher **ABV (8–10%+)** and **bitterness (70–100+ IBU)**
- **Crystal clear** with a massive, resinous hop character
- Often paired with **DDH** for maximum aroma + bitterness
- Best enjoyed **fresh** and **cold** — this is not a cellar beer
