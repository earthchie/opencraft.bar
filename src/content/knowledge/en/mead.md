# 🍯 Mead

<div class="article-lead">

Mead is a fermented drink made primarily from **honey, water, and yeast**. It is often described as a kind of "honey wine," but in practice it stands in its own category and can range from still to sparkling, dry to sweet, and simple to highly elaborate.

Although mead is one of the oldest known fermented beverages, many of the most dramatic claims about it are difficult to prove precisely. What is clear is that people in many regions discovered that honey could ferment and turned that into a wide range of drinks.

</div>

---

## 🏯 A Brief History

Versions of mead or honey fermentation appear in many ancient societies:

- **Ancient China** — archaeological finds show fermented beverages that included honey among other ingredients
- **Northern Europe** — mead appears in myth, poetry, and ritual traditions
- **Ancient Mediterranean cultures** — honey-fermented drinks were known in various forms
- **Medieval Europe** — mead persisted both as a domestic drink and as part of courtly or ceremonial culture

The popular link between mead and the word **"honeymoon"** is often repeated, though the exact historical path of the term is not fully settled. It does, however, reflect the long association between honey, marriage, and celebration.

In recent decades, mead has seen renewed interest as modern meaderies experiment with fruit, spice, hops, barrel aging, and different sweetness levels.

## 👅 What Does It Taste Like?

The flavor of mead varies widely depending on the honey variety, fermentation choices, alcohol level, sweetness, and any added fruit, spice, or botanicals.

<div class="taste-box">

### 🎨 Appearance
- Pale straw to deep amber or gold
- Can be clear or hazy
- Can be still, lightly sparkling, or fully sparkling

### 👃 Aroma
- **Honey-derived aroma** — floral, waxy, herbal, or sweet depending on source
- **Depending on additions**: fruit, spice, herbs, flowers, oak, or even hops
- Clean fermentation — no off-notes

### 👄 Flavor
- **Honey character** — varies by floral source (wildflower, orange blossom, clover, etc.)
- **Sweet to dry** — depends on residual sugar
- **Usually low bitterness** — unless hops, spices, oak, or tannic fruit are involved
- **Fruit, spice, or herbal notes** — if added
- Finish can be dry, crisp, rounded, tannic, or rich depending on style

### ✨ Mouthfeel
- **Light to full body** — varies widely
- Can be still, petillant (lightly sparkling), or fully carbonated
- Some meads have a slight tannic grip (from fruit or aging)

</div>

## 🍶 Types of Mead

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Traditional Mead** | Honey, water, yeast — focused on the base honey character |
| **Melomel** | Mead with fruit (berries, cherries, citrus, etc.) |
| **Metheglin** | Mead with spices or herbs (cinnamon, ginger, vanilla) |
| **Bochet** | Mead made with caramelized honey — deeper, toasty flavor |
| **Cyser** | Mead made with apples (like honey + apple cider) |
| **Pyment** | Mead made with grapes (like honey + wine) |
| **Hydromel** | Lower-alcohol mead — lighter and often more refreshing |
| **Sack Mead** | Higher-alcohol mead — usually richer and fuller |
| **Braggot** | Mead made with malted grain — a hybrid of mead and beer |
| **Hopped Mead** | Mead dry-hopped like an IPA — honey + hops |

## 🍯 Honey: The Soul of Mead

Honey variety is one of the biggest influences on flavor:

| Honey Source | Flavor Profile |
|---|---|
| **Orange Blossom** | Light, citrusy, floral |
| **Wildflower** | Complex, earthy, floral |
| **Clover** | Mild, sweet, clean |
| **Buckwheat** | Dark, molasses-like, earthy |
| **Manuka** | Herbal, medicinal, intense |
| **Acacia** | Very light, delicate, floral |

## 🍽️ Food Pairing

Mead can be very flexible at the table, but the pairing depends heavily on sweetness, carbonation, and what else is in the recipe.

| Mead Type | Food Pairing |
|---|---|
| **Dry Traditional** | Charcuterie, soft cheeses, roasted chicken |
| **Sweet Traditional** | Blue cheese, fruit tarts, crème brûlée |
| **Melomel (Berry)** | Dark chocolate, grilled meats, berry desserts |
| **Melomel (Citrus)** | Seafood, salads, spicy dishes |
| **Metheglin (Spiced)** | Roasted pork, spiced cakes, mulled wine dishes |
| **Hopped Mead** | Burgers, fried food, spicy cuisine |
| **Braggot** | BBQ, aged cheese, hearty stews |

## 💡 Tips for Enjoying

- **Temperature matters** — lighter meads often show well cooler; richer or stronger meads can open up a bit warmer
- **Give strong meads a moment** — especially if they are high in alcohol or have been aged
- **Pair by style, not by category** — a dry traditional mead behaves very differently from a sweet melomel or a braggot
- **Do not expect it to behave exactly like beer or wine** — mead has its own balance of honey, acidity, tannin, and alcohol
- **Sparkling mead can work in similar occasions to sparkling wine** — but it is still its own style rather than a direct substitute
