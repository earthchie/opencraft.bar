# ☁️ Hazy IPA

<div class="article-lead">

Hazy IPA — often also called New England IPA (NEIPA) — is a modern IPA style built around **soft texture, saturated hop aroma, and visible haze**. Compared with a classic West Coast IPA, it usually puts less emphasis on sharp bitterness and more on fruit-forward hop character.

Typical examples show a cloudy appearance, expressive hop aroma, and a smoother mouthfeel than many clearer IPA styles.

</div>

---

## 🏯 A Brief History

The rise of Hazy IPA is usually traced to the northeastern United States in the early 2010s. Breweries such as The Alchemist and Tree House helped popularize unfiltered, heavily hop-saturated IPAs that looked cloudy and tasted softer than classic West Coast examples.

The style spread quickly through the craft beer world. Drinkers responded to the intense hop aroma, softer bitterness, and fuller texture.

Today, Hazy IPA is one of the most widely recognized modern craft beer styles.

## 👅 What Does It Taste Like?

<div class="taste-box">

### 🎨 Appearance
- **Hazy to opaque** — often visibly cloudy
- Pale straw to deep gold
- Fluffy white head, good retention

### 👃 Aroma
- **Intensely fruity** — mango, passion fruit, pineapple, citrus, peach
- Hop aroma often leans away from sharp pine or aggressive resin, though some examples still show herbal or dank notes
- Faint cereal or oat sweetness in the background
- No caramel or toasted notes

### 👄 Flavor
- **Juicy, fruit-forward hop flavor** — the defining characteristic
- **Lower-perceived bitterness** than many West Coast IPAs — often around 30–50 IBU, though the exact number varies
- **Soft, pillowy base** — often supported by oats or wheat
- Bitterness is usually present, but smoother and rounder than in sharper IPA substyles
- **Semi-dry to soft finish** with lingering hop character

### ✨ Mouthfeel
- **Medium to medium-full body** — often fuller than a standard pale IPA
- **Soft, creamy, almost pillowy** — a common hallmark of the style
- Moderate carbonation, gentle on the palate

</div>

## 🍶 What Makes Hazy IPA Different?

| Feature | Hazy IPA | West Coast IPA |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance | Hazy, opaque | Brilliantly clear |
| Bitterness | Low, smooth (30–50 IBU) | High, sharp (50–70+ IBU) |
| Body | Full, soft, creamy | Medium, crisp |
| Hops | Late additions and heavy dry hopping | Bittering plus late hops |
| Grains | Oats / wheat added | Mostly barley malt |
| Mouthfeel | Pillowy, juicy | Dry, clean |

### The Secret: Oats and Dry Hopping

Two things make Hazy IPA unique:

1. **Oats (or wheat)** in the grain bill contribute proteins and texture, helping create both haze and a softer mouthfeel.

2. **Heavy dry hopping** — hops are added late or after fermentation to emphasize aroma and flavor without creating the same kind of sharp bitterness associated with earlier boil additions.

### The Haze-Texture Connection

Unlike some styles where haze is accidental, in Hazy IPA a cloudy appearance is usually expected. That haze comes from the interaction of proteins, polyphenols, yeast, and hop matter. It can contribute to texture and hop presentation, but it does **not** make the beer resistant to oxidation — in fact, Hazy IPA is often one of the styles most sensitive to oxygen pickup.

## 🍽️ Food Pairing

The softer bitterness and fruit-forward hop character of Hazy IPA make it very approachable with food.

| Food | Why It Works |
|---|---|
| 🍔 **Burgers** | Juicy hops complement beef, soft body balances richness |
| 🌮 **Tacos / Mexican** | Citrusy hops love lime, salsa, and spice |
| 🍕 **Pizza** | Carbonation cuts through cheese, fruit notes brighten tomato |
| 🐔 **Fried Chicken** | Soft bitterness + carbonation = perfect for fried food |
| 🥭 **Spicy Thai / Asian** | Juicy sweetness cools heat, doesn't overpower flavors |
| 🧀 **Mild Cheeses** | Soft body complements instead of competing |

## 💡 Tips for Enjoying

- **Drink relatively fresh** — Hazy IPA changes quickly with time, especially in cans or bottles. Packaged examples are usually best while still fresh; draught versions kept well under CO2 can hold up better.
- **Serve cool, not ice-cold** — 10–12°C lets hop aroma show more clearly
- **Do not judge it by clarity alone** — haze is common in the style, but good Hazy IPA still depends on balance, freshness, and stable brewing
- **Try it next to a West Coast IPA** — the contrast in bitterness, texture, and hop presentation is one of the easiest ways to understand both styles
