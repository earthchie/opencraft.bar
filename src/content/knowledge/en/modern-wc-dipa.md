# 🍯 Modern WC DIPA

<div class="article-lead">

Modern WC DIPA (West Coast Double IPA) is a modern interpretation of the West Coast Double IPA tradition. It keeps the core West Coast traits — **clarity, firm bitterness, and prominent hop character** — but usually with a cleaner fermentation profile, less sweetness, and less crystal/caramel malt character than older examples.

In practical terms, it is a **stronger, drier, more hop-forward West Coast IPA** with a larger malt base to support the alcohol and bitterness.

</div>

---

## 🏯 What Defines Modern WC DIPA?

The "Double" in DIPA originally suggested a bigger, more heavily hopped IPA. In modern use, it usually means a beer with:

- **Higher ABV** — typically 7.5-9.5%
- **More hop bitterness and flavor** — often 60-100+ IBU
- **A larger malt base** — to balance the alcohol and hop load

The "Modern" part usually means: **less crystal/caramel malt, cleaner fermentation, and a drier finish** than older West Coast DIPAs. That shift reduces toffee-like sweetness and lets the hop profile come through more clearly.

| Feature | Standard IPA | Modern WC DIPA |
|---|---|---|
| ABV | 5.5-7.0% | 7.5-9.5% |
| IBU (Bitterness) | 40-70 | 60-100+ |
| Body | Medium | Medium-full |
| Malt character | Subtle | Firm but clean |
| Hop intensity | Bold | Very high |
| Finish | Dry | Dry with lingering bitterness |

## 👅 What Does It Taste Like?

<div class="taste-box">

### 🎨 Appearance
- Clear to brilliant — from deep gold to light amber
- Dense white head with good retention
- Usually bright and polished in appearance

### 👃 Aroma
- **Strong hop aroma** — pine, resin, grapefruit, orange zest, and floral notes
- **Subtle malt presence** — bread, cracker, or light toast rather than obvious caramel sweetness
- **Faint alcohol warmth** — noticeable in stronger examples
- Clean fermentation — no off-notes

### 👄 Flavor
- **Hop-forward flavor** — pine, citrus, resin, grapefruit, and sometimes dank or tropical notes
- **Firm bitterness** — assertive, but ideally clean rather than rough
- **Clean malt backbone** — enough body to support the hops without turning sweet or heavy
- **Less crystal/caramel character** — compared with older West Coast DIPAs, the finish is usually drier
- **Dry finish** — bitterness often lingers, especially in classic West Coast examples

### ✨ Mouthfeel
- **Medium-full body** — fuller than a standard IPA, but not syrupy
- **Smooth overall** — the best examples feel structured rather than heavy
- Moderate carbonation
- **Slight alcohol warmth** — pleasant

</div>

## 🍶 West Coast vs. New England DIPA

| Aspect | Modern WC DIPA | Hazy / NE DIPA |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance | Clear | Hazy |
| Bitterness | High, firm | Low, smooth |
| Hop character | Pine, resin, citrus | Tropical fruit, juicy |
| Malt | Clean, supportive | Soft, oat-y |
| Body | Medium-full, crisp edge | Full, pillowy |
| ABV | 7.5-9.5% | 6.5-8.5% |

## 🍽️ Food Pairing

Modern WC DIPA works best with food that can handle high bitterness and alcohol.

| Food | Why It Works |
|---|---|
| 🥩 **Grilled Steak** | Hop bitterness cuts through char and fat |
| 🧀 **Aged Cheddar** | Bold hops match bold cheese |
| 🌶️ **Spicy Wings** | Carbonation and bitterness balance heat |
| 🍔 **Double Cheeseburger** | Rich meat and cheese stand up to the beer's bitterness |
| 🥓 **BBQ Ribs** | Smoky sweetness meets hoppy bitterness |
| 🍫 **Dark Chocolate** | The bitterness of both complements each other |

## 💡 Tips for Enjoying

- **Serve cool, not ice-cold** — around 10-13°C helps the aroma and bitterness show more clearly
- **Drink it slowly** — at 7.5%+ ABV, this is not a fast-drinking beer
- **Food helps** — the bitterness and alcohol usually show better alongside richer dishes
- **Freshness matters** — hop aroma changes with time, so most examples are best while still relatively fresh
- **Not usually a long-aging style** — some bottles may evolve, but most are intended to be consumed fresh
